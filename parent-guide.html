How Pack 3963 Communicates

We use a combination of email, Scoutbook, and our Pack website to keep families informed. Major announcements are posted on the homepage banner and emailed to all registered parents.

  • Email — weekly updates, schedule changes, and reminders
  • Scoutbook — advancement tracking and den communication
  • Website — event details, resources, and Pack-wide updates

Meeting Expectations

Pack 3963 generally meets weekly unless otherwise noted. Meetings may include den activities, advancement work, Pack activities, awards, service projects, or preparation for upcoming events. Schedule changes and weeks without a meeting will be shared with families in advance.

  • Arrive on time and ready to participate
  • A parent, guardian, or responsible adult must remain present for every Scout during every meeting
  • Formal uniforms are not required for regular den meetings
  • Respectful and safe behavior is expected from Scouts and adults
  • Check the Pack calendar and weekly emails for the latest schedule

Uniform Guide

Formal uniforms are not required for regular den meetings. A full uniform is typically expected for special events, ceremonies, and other designated activities. Pack leadership will tell families ahead of time whenever a full uniform is requested.

  • Lion — T-shirt and optional cap
  • Tiger — Blue uniform shirt, neckerchief, and slide
  • Wolf — Blue uniform shirt, neckerchief, and slide
  • Bear — Blue uniform shirt, neckerchief, and slide
  • Webelos — Tan uniform shirt and neckerchief
  • Arrow of Light — Tan uniform shirt and neckerchief

Full uniform details are available on our Resources page.

Advancement Overview

Scouts earn adventure loops and pins throughout the year. Advancement is tracked in Scoutbook and recognized at Pack meetings and ceremonies.

  • Adventure loops and pins
  • Rank requirements
  • Pack awards and recognition

Parent Participation

Parents and guardians are essential to the success of our Pack. In addition to remaining present with their Scout at meetings, adults can help with den activities, volunteer at events, share a skill, or join the Pack committee.

Safety & Youth Protection

All registered leaders are trained in Youth Protection. A parent, guardian, or responsible adult must supervise their Scout throughout every meeting. Families should also follow the activity-specific safety directions shared by Pack leadership.
